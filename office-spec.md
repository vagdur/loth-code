# Specification of the Liturgy of the Hours

A working specification, derived from the GILH, sufficient to determine the exact contents of the Office on any given day. Paragraph numbers in parentheses refer to the GILH.

---

## 1. The Hours

The Office consists of the following Hours:

| Hour | Name | Approx. time |
|---|---|---|
| Invitatory | (introduction to whole Office) | first Hour of the day |
| Office of Readings | — | any time (or night) |
| Lauds | Morning Prayer | morning |
| Terce | Before Noon | mid-morning |
| Sext | Midday | midday |
| None | Afternoon | mid-afternoon |
| Vespers | Evening Prayer | evening |
| Compline | Night Prayer | before bed |

Terce, Sext, and None collectively are called "Daytime Prayer" or "the Prayer During the Day."

---

## 2. Books / Sources of Texts

Texts are drawn from the following parts of the Office:

- **Psalter** — a four-week cycle (Weeks I–IV), each day having its own psalmody, antiphons, hymns, short readings, ferial concluding prayers, and intercessions.
- **Proper of Seasons** — texts assigned to days of Advent, Christmas, Lent, Holy Week, Easter Triduum, Eastertide, and Ordinary Time.
- **Proper of Saints** — texts for solemnities, feasts, and memorias of saints.
- **Commons** — generic texts for categories of saints (Apostles, Martyrs, Pastors, Virgins, Holy Women, BVM, etc.) used when proper texts are absent.
- **Supplement / Optional Lectionary** — psalm-prayers; alternative two-year cycle of biblical readings; alternative patristic readings (161, 250).

When "the Proper" is referenced without qualification, the day's own proper texts are meant; if none exist, fall back to the relevant Common (224).

---

## 3. Structure of Each Hour

### 3.1 Invitatory (34–36)

Said once per day, prefixed to whichever Hour begins the day (Office of Readings, or Lauds if OoR is omitted/said earlier).

1. Verse: *Lord, open our lips: And we shall praise your name* (with sign of cross on the lips).
2. **Psalm 94** with its antiphon (or Ps 99, 66, or 23 may replace it). Said responsorially: antiphon first, repeated, and again after each verse.

If the invitatory is followed immediately by Lauds, the introductory verse of Lauds is omitted (41). The psalm with antiphon may be omitted when it precedes Lauds (35).

The invitatory antiphon varies by liturgical day (proper or seasonal).

### 3.2 Lauds and Vespers (41–54)

1. **Introductory verse**: *O God, come to our aid: O Lord, make haste to help us. Glory be to the Father… Alleluia.*
   - The *Alleluia* is omitted in Lent.
   - This whole verse is omitted if the invitatory immediately precedes Lauds.
2. **Hymn** — proper to the Hour, season, or feast.
3. **Psalmody**:
   - **Lauds**: 1 morning psalm + 1 Old Testament canticle + 1 psalm of praise.
   - **Vespers**: 2 psalms (or sections of longer psalms) + 1 New Testament canticle from the Epistles or Revelation.
   - Each item has its own antiphon. *Glory be* concludes each psalm/canticle.
4. **Reading**:
   - Normally a short reading proper to the day/season/feast (varies with daily psalmody).
   - A longer reading may be substituted, especially in celebrations with the people (46).
5. *(Optional)* Brief homily (47).
6. *(Optional)* Silent pause (48).
7. **Responsory** (short responsory, or other approved song); may be omitted (49).
8. **Gospel Canticle** with antiphon:
   - Lauds: ***Benedictus***.
   - Vespers: ***Magnificat***.
   - Antiphons vary by day/season/feast.
9. **Intercessions**:
   - Lauds: invocations consecrating the day to God.
   - Vespers: petitions; the **final intention is always for the dead** (186).
10. **Lord's Prayer** — said by all, optionally preceded by a short introduction.
11. **Concluding prayer** — see §5.4.
12. **Dismissal** (in communal celebration with priest/deacon: *The Lord be with you* + blessing + *Go in the peace of Christ*; otherwise *The Lord bless us…*).

### 3.3 Office of Readings (55–69)

1. **If it is the first Hour of the day**: the Invitatory (§3.1) precedes.
   **Otherwise**: introductory verse *O God, come to our aid… Glory be… Alleluia* (Alleluia omitted in Lent).
2. **Hymn** — two series exist for Ordinary Time: one for night use and one for day use; choose by the actual time of recitation (58, 61, 176).
3. **Psalmody**: 3 psalms (or sections) with antiphons.
   - On the Easter Triduum, octaves of Easter and Christmas, and on solemnities and feasts: psalms and antiphons are **proper** (62).
   - On Sundays, ferial days, and memorias: from the current psalter (62), unless memoria has proper psalms.
4. **Versicle** (transition to readings).
5. **First Reading** — biblical, with its **responsory**.
   - Normally from the Proper of Season (66).
   - On solemnities and feasts: from the Proper or Common.
6. **Second Reading** — patristic / ecclesiastical writer / hagiographical, with its responsory.
   - Normally from the Proper of Season (67).
   - On solemnities and feasts: a proper hagiographical reading; if none, from the Common.
   - On memorias: a proper hagiographical reading replaces the patristic reading of the current ferial day; if no proper is assigned, the ferial patristic reading is retained. When a hagiographical reading is used, its responsory may be proper or from the Common. Cf. dubium, *Notitiae* 12 (1976), 46.
   - May alternatively be drawn from the Optional Lectionary (161).
7. ***Te Deum***:
   - **Said** on Sundays (outside Lent), the octaves of Easter and Christmas, solemnities, and feasts.
   - **Omitted** on memorias and ferial days.
   - Final part (from *Lord, save your people*…) may be omitted.
8. **Concluding prayer** of the day.
9. **Acclamation** *Let us praise the Lord: Thanks be to God* (in common recitation).

#### Vigil extension (Sundays, solemnities, feasts) (70–73)

When a more extended celebration is desired, after both readings and before the *Te Deum*:
- Add canticles (from the appropriate appendix).
- Read a Gospel passage:
  - Solemnities and feasts: from the Mass Lectionary.
  - Sundays: from the paschal-mystery series in the Appendix.
- *(Optional)* homily.
- Then *Te Deum* and concluding prayer.

### 3.4 Daytime Prayer — Terce, Sext, None (79–83)

Three Hours of identical structure. If only one is said outside choir, choose the one corresponding to the actual time of day (77).

1. **Introductory verse** *O God, come to our aid… Glory be… Alleluia* (Alleluia omitted in Lent).
2. **Hymn** corresponding to the Hour.
3. **Psalmody**: 3 psalms (or parts) with antiphons.
   - Two psalmodies are provided: the **current** psalmody (from the four-week psalter) and the **complementary** psalmody (chosen from the gradual psalms).
   - If only one Hour is said: use the current psalmody.
   - If two or three Hours are said: use the current psalmody for one (the one corresponding to the time of day), the complementary psalmody for the others.
   - On solemnities, the Easter Triduum, and the octave of Easter: proper antiphons + 3 psalms drawn from the complementary psalms — *unless* special psalms are indicated, or a solemnity falls on a Sunday (in which case psalms are from Sunday Week I) (82).
4. **Short reading** — varies by liturgical day, season, or feast.
5. **Versicle**.
6. **Concluding prayer** (see §5.4).
7. **Acclamation** *Let us praise the Lord: Thanks be to God*.

### 3.5 Compline (84–92)

1. **Introductory verse** *O God, come to our aid… Glory be… Alleluia* (Alleluia omitted in Lent).
2. *(Optional)* Examination of conscience (silent, or one of the Missal's penitential acts in common recitation).
3. **Hymn**.
4. **Psalmody**:
   - After **First Vespers of Sunday**: Ps 4 and Ps 133.
   - After **Second Vespers of Sunday**: Ps 90.
   - On other days: psalms expressing confidence in God, given in the psalter for each day. The Sunday psalms may always be substituted (helpful for those reciting from memory).
5. **Short reading**.
6. **Responsory** *Into your hands*.
7. **Gospel canticle** (***Nunc dimittis***) with antiphon — the culmination of the Hour.
8. **Concluding prayer** — always taken from the psalter (198).
9. **Blessing** *The Lord grant us a quiet night* (said even in individual recitation).
10. **Marian antiphon** — one of the seasonal Marian antiphons. In **Eastertide** the antiphon is always *Regina caeli*.

## 4. The Liturgical Calendar — Classes of Days

Days are classified in descending order of rank:

1. **Easter Triduum** (Holy Thursday evening through Easter Sunday evening).
2. **Solemnities** — including all Sundays of Advent, Lent, and Eastertide (which take the Sunday rank but follow Sunday rules); other solemnities of the Lord, of the BVM, and of saints.
3. **Feasts of the Lord** falling on a Sunday in Ordinary Time.
4. **Sundays** of Christmastide and Ordinary Time.
5. **Feasts** (other than the above).
6. **Memorias**, obligatory or optional.
7. **Ferial days** within privileged seasons (Advent ferial of 17–24 Dec, Christmas octave, Lenten ferial, Holy Week ferial, Eastertide ferial).
8. **Ordinary ferial days**.

Only solemnities are transferable (222). On any given date, the highest-ranking observance determines the Office, except where memoria-overlay rules below apply.

## 5. How Texts Are Chosen — Rules by Day Class

### 5.1 Sundays (204–207)

- Begin with **First Vespers** on the preceding (Saturday) evening. Everything is from the psalter except texts proper to the Sunday.
- If a feast of the Lord falls on a Sunday, **the feast has its own First Vespers** (replacing Sunday's) (205).
- Sunday Vigil per §3.3 vigil extension (206).
- Psalms always express the paschal mystery (129).

### 5.2 Solemnities (225–230)

- Have **First Vespers** on the preceding day.
- **First & Second Vespers**: hymn, antiphons, short reading + responsory, concluding prayer — all proper (else from Common).
  - First Vespers psalms: from the **Laudate series** (Ps 112, 116, 134, 145, 146, 147), in the order given. NT canticle indicated in place.
  - Second Vespers: psalms and canticle proper.
  - Intercessions: proper or from Common.
- **Lauds**: hymn, antiphons, short reading + responsory, concluding prayer — proper (else Common). Psalms from **Sunday Week I**. Intercessions: proper or Common.
- **Office of Readings**: everything proper — hymns, antiphons, psalms, readings (1st biblical, 2nd hagiographical), responsories. ***Te Deum*** + proper prayer.
  - For a saint of merely local cult lacking special texts even in the local Proper: everything from the Common.
- **Daytime Prayer**: hymn of ferial day; gradual psalms with proper antiphon; on Sundays, psalms from Sunday Week I; short reading and concluding prayer proper. Certain solemnities of the Lord have special psalms.
- **Compline**: from the Sunday — after First Vespers use Sunday First Vespers Compline; after Second Vespers use Sunday Second Vespers Compline.

If a Sunday is occupied by a solemnity occurring on it, the solemnity's psalms are still from Sunday Week I (82).

### 5.3 Feasts (231–233)

- **No First Vespers**, *unless* it is a feast of the Lord falling on a Sunday (in which case First Vespers replaces the Sunday's).
- **Office of Readings, Lauds, Vespers**: as on a solemnity.
- **Daytime Prayer**: hymn of the day; psalms with antiphons of the ferial day (unless a special reason or tradition requires proper antiphons — indicated in place); short reading and concluding prayer from the Proper.
- **Compline**: as on ordinary days.

### 5.4 Memorias on Ordinary Days (234–236)

Obligatory and optional memorias are arranged identically; the only difference is that an optional memoria may be omitted (220). If multiple optional memorias coincide, only one is celebrated (221).

In the **Office of Readings, Lauds, and Vespers**:

| Element | Source |
|---|---|
| Psalms with antiphons | Current ferial day, *unless* proper antiphons or psalms are indicated |
| Invitatory antiphon, hymn, short reading, antiphons at *Benedictus* / *Magnificat*, intercessions | If proper, from the Office of the saint; otherwise from the Common or the current ferial day |
| Concluding prayer | From the Office of the saint |
| OoR 1st reading + responsory | Scripture reading of the ferial day |
| OoR 2nd reading | Proper hagiographical reading (responsory proper or from Common); if no proper, the patristic reading of the current ferial day — not the Common (*Notitiae* 12 (1976), 46) |
| ***Te Deum*** | **Not said** |

In **Daytime Prayer and Compline**: nothing is taken from the Office of the saint; everything is from the ferial day.

### 5.5 Memorias During Privileged Seasons (237–240)

A **privileged season** for memoria purposes means: Sundays, solemnities, feasts, Ash Wednesday, Holy Week, and the octave of Easter — and additionally for memoria-suppression, the ferial days between 17 and 24 December, the octave of Christmas, and the ferial days of Lent.

- On Sundays, solemnities, feasts, Ash Wednesday, Holy Week, octave of Easter: **no memoria is commemorated, even if it falls on that day** (237).
- On 17–24 Dec ferials, Christmas octave, Lent ferials: **no obligatory memoria may be celebrated, not even from particular calendars**; memorias of Lent are treated as optional that year (238).
- If, during these seasons, the optional commemoration of a saint is desired (239):
  - **Office of Readings**: after the patristic reading + responsory from the Proper of Season, add the proper hagiographical reading + responsory; conclude with the prayer of the saint.
  - **Lauds and Vespers**: after the concluding prayer, the antiphon of the saint (proper or Common) and the prayer of the saint may be added.

### 5.6 Saturday Memoria of the BVM (240)

On Saturdays in Ordinary Time on which optional memorias are permitted, the optional memoria of the BVM may be celebrated in the same manner as a memoria on an ordinary day (§5.4), with proper readings from the BVM Common.

### 5.7 Ferial Days

- **Office of Readings, Lauds, Vespers, Daytime Prayer, Compline**: all from the psalter for that day in conjunction with the Proper of Season for the relevant items (short readings, antiphons in privileged seasons, etc.).
- Specifically: Lauds and Vespers use proper antiphons during the ferial days of Holy Week, Eastertide, and 17–24 Dec, and on Sundays of Advent/Christmas/Lent/Eastertide (116).

## 6. Concluding Prayers — Source by Day (197–200)

| Hour | Day | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Office of Readings | Always | Proper of the day |
| Lauds & Vespers | Sundays; ferial days of Advent, Christmas, Lent, Eastertide; solemnities; feasts; memorias | Proper |
| Lauds & Vespers | Ordinary ferial days | Psalter |
| Daytime Prayer | Sundays; ferial days of Advent, Christmas, Lent, Eastertide; solemnities; feasts | Proper |
| Daytime Prayer | Other days | Psalter |
| Compline | Always | Psalter |

## 7. Antiphons (116–120)

- **Lauds and Vespers** have proper antiphons:
  - Throughout the Easter Triduum.
  - In the octaves of Easter and Christmas.
  - On Sundays of Advent, Christmas, Lent, and Eastertide.
  - On the ferial days of Holy Week, of Eastertide, and of 17–24 December.
- **Solemnities**: proper antiphons at the Office of Readings, Lauds, Daytime Prayer, and Vespers (or from the Common if not).
- **Feasts**: proper antiphons at the Office of Readings, Lauds, and Vespers (or from the Common).
- **Benedictus and Magnificat antiphons**:
  - Seasonal Office: from the Proper of Season if any, else from the current psalter.
  - Solemnities and feasts: from the Proper, else from the Common.
  - Memorias (if not having proper antiphons): from the Common, or from the current ferial day.
  - **Three-year Sunday cycle** (*Liturgia Horarum* editio typica altera, 1985–87): on Sundays (and other days that take the Sunday gospel), the Proper of Season may assign a distinct gospel-canticle antiphon for lectionary Years A, B, and C. When cycle-specific texts are present, the antiphon of the current Sunday cycle is used; otherwise the single (non-cycled) proper antiphon remains the fallback.
- **Eastertide**: *Alleluia* is appended to every antiphon, unless it clashes with the antiphon's meaning.

---

## 8. Short Readings (157–158)

- For Ordinary Time: a four-week cycle in the psalter (varies the reading every four weeks).
- For Advent, Christmas, Lent, Eastertide: variation on a single-week basis.
- Solemnities, feasts, and certain memorias have proper short readings.
- Compline has its own one-week series.
- Gospels are excluded; Vespers short readings are from the New Testament (since the canticle is Marian).

---

## 9. Hymns (173–177)

- For Ordinary Time, two series for each Hour, used on alternate weeks.
- For the Office of Readings, two series in Ordinary Time depending on whether it is recited at night or during the day.
- On solemnities, feasts, and proper days, hymns are from the Proper or, if absent, from the Common.

---

## 10. Psalter — Four-Week Cycle Alignment with the Year (133–135)

- The cycle's first week (Week I) begins on:
  - The **First Sunday of Advent**;
  - The **First Sunday of Ordinary Time**;
  - The **First Sunday of Lent**;
  - **Easter Sunday**.
  - Any remaining weeks of the cycle preceding these Sundays are simply omitted.
- After **Pentecost**, the cycle resumes from the week indicated at the start of the corresponding week in the Proper of Season.
- On solemnities, the Easter Triduum, and the octaves of Easter and Christmas, **proper psalms** are used at the Office of Readings.
- At Lauds, the psalms and canticle of **Sunday Week I** are used on solemnities.
- **First Vespers of solemnities**: psalms from the Laudate series; NT canticle as indicated.
- **Second Vespers of solemnities, and Vespers of feasts**: psalms and canticle proper.
- **Daytime Prayer on solemnities** (except those mentioned above and when not on a Sunday): the **gradual psalms** are used. On Sundays — Sunday Week I. On feasts — psalms of the current ferial day.

### Special psalter notes

- Ps 77, Ps 104, Ps 105 are reserved for Advent, Christmas, Lent, and Eastertide (130).
- Ps 57, Ps 82, Ps 108 (and certain verses of others) are omitted entirely from the psalter (131); omitted verses are noted at the start of each affected psalm.
- Ps 118, divided across 22 days, is used at Daytime Prayer (132).
- Penitential / Passion psalms are assigned to Fridays (129).
- Sunday psalms throughout the four weeks are chosen to express the paschal mystery (129).

---

## 11. Canticles

- **Lauds OT canticles**: between the first and second psalm. A four-week cycle of ferial canticles assigns a different canticle to each weekday; on Sundays the two parts of the Canticle of the Three Children are used alternately (136).
- **Vespers NT canticles**: from the Epistles or Revelation; one for each day of the week.
  - On Sundays of **Lent**: the Alleluia-canticle from Revelation is replaced by a canticle from 1 Peter.
  - On the **Epiphany** and the **Transfiguration**: the canticle is from 1 Timothy (137).
- **Gospel canticles** (*Benedictus*, *Magnificat*, *Nunc dimittis*) are accorded the dignity of the Gospel (138).

---

## 12. Office of Readings — Biblical Reading Cycle (143–155)

- A **one-year cycle** is in the standard Office.
- A **two-year cycle** (Year I / Year II) is in the Supplement; either may be used. The Gospels are not read (read at Mass).
- The two-year arrangement (Year I = odd years, Year II = even):

| Period | Year I | Year II |
|---|---|---|
| Advent | Isaiah (semi-continuous, 2-yr) + Ruth + Micah; 17–24 Dec has special readings; readings of any unused 3rd-week-of-Advent are omitted | (same Isaiah pattern, alternate-year) |
| 29 Dec – 5 Jan | Colossians | Song of Songs |
| 7 Jan – Saturday after Epiphany | Isaiah 60–66 + Baruch | (same; texts otherwise unused are omitted that year) |
| Lent | Deuteronomy + Hebrews | Exodus, Leviticus, Numbers |
| Holy Week | 3rd & 4th Servant Songs + Lamentations | Jeremiah |
| Eastertide (except Easter Sundays I–II, Ascension, Pentecost) | 1 Peter, 1–3 John, Revelation | Acts |
| Ordinary Time | Historical books from Joshua to Exile, with prophets interleaved | Genesis, then post-Exile through Maccabees; later prophets, wisdom books, Esther, Tobit, Judith |

- Continuous reading runs through 34 weeks of Ordinary Time; the series is interrupted from Ash Wednesday to Pentecost.
- After Pentecost, readings resume from the week following the one interrupted by Lent, omitting the Sunday readings of that week (152).
- In years with only 33 ordinary weeks, the week immediately after Pentecost is omitted, so the eschatological end-of-year readings are preserved (152).
- Solemnities and feasts have proper readings; otherwise taken from the Common (154).

---

## 13. Patristic / Hagiographical Readings (159–168)

- Office of Readings 2nd reading is normally patristic (Fathers, Doctors, ecclesiastical writers).
- May be taken from the standard Office or the Optional Lectionary (161).
- On memorias: a proper hagiographical reading is used in place of the current patristic reading; if no proper is assigned, the patristic reading of the ferial day is retained (not the Common). When a hagiographical reading is used, its responsory may be proper or from the Common. See dubium, *Notitiae* 12 (1976), 46.
- A short biographical note precedes hagiographical readings; it is **not read aloud** (168).

---

## 14. Responsories

- The biblical reading at the Office of Readings is followed by its own responsory (169).
- The patristic / hagiographical reading is followed by its responsory (170).
- Lauds, Vespers, and Compline have a short responsory after the short reading (172). It may be omitted (49).
- Daytime Prayer has a versicle (not a responsory) after the short reading.

---

## 15. Joining Hours with Mass (93–99)

When an Hour publicly precedes or follows Mass of the same Office:

### Lauds → Mass (94)
- Begin with either Lauds' introductory verse + hymn (especially ferials), or the entrance song + procession + greeting (especially festive days). Use one only.
- Lauds psalmody to the short reading exclusively.
- Mass penitential act omitted; Kyrie omitted if desired; Gloria as required; opening prayer of Mass.
- Liturgy of the Word as usual. On a ferial morning Mass, the intercessions of Lauds may replace the Prayer of the Faithful.
- After communion: *Benedictus* with antiphon, then postcommunion and remainder of Mass.

### Daytime Prayer → Mass (95)
- As above; psalmody to before the short reading; then Kyrie / Gloria / opening prayer.

### Vespers → Mass (96)
- As Lauds. **First Vespers** of solemnities, Sundays, and feasts of the Lord on Sundays may NOT be celebrated until after the Mass of the previous day or the Saturday.

### Mass → Daytime Prayer / Vespers (97)
- Mass to the postcommunion inclusively.
- Hour begins without introductory verse, immediately after postcommunion.
- **Daytime Prayer**: short reading is omitted; concluding prayer; dismissal as at Mass.
- **Vespers**: *Magnificat* with antiphon immediately after the psalmody — no reading, no intercessions, no Lord's Prayer — then concluding prayer + blessing.

### Office of Readings + Mass (98)
- Generally NOT joined, except **Christmas Night**.
- When joined: Mass begins immediately after the second reading + responsory of the Office, omitting everything before *Gloria in excelsis* (if to be said) or the opening prayer.

### Office of Readings + Another Hour (99)
- When OoR immediately precedes another Hour, a hymn fitting that other Hour may be used to begin the Office of Readings.
- The closing prayer + acclamation of the Office of Readings, and the introductory verse + *Glory be* of the following Hour, are both omitted.

## 16. Easter Triduum Specifics (208–213)

- Office is celebrated as in the Proper of the season.
- Those attending the **evening Mass on Holy Thursday** or the **Passion of the Lord on Good Friday** do not say Vespers of that day.
- Before morning Lauds on **Good Friday and Holy Saturday**, the Office of Readings is, where possible, celebrated publicly with the people.
- **Compline of Holy Saturday** is said only by those not present at the Easter Vigil.
- The **Easter Vigil takes the place of the Office of Readings**; those not present should choose at least four of its readings with their canticles and prayers (suitably: Exodus, Ezekiel, the Apostle, the Gospel), followed by *Te Deum* and the prayer of the day.
- **Easter Sunday Lauds** are said by all. Vespers should be celebrated more solemnly. Where the custom exists, **Baptismal Vespers** with a procession to the font during the singing of the psalms may be celebrated.

---

## 17. Christmas Specifics (215–216)

- Before the **Midnight Mass of Christmas**, a vigil is appropriately celebrated using the Office of Readings.
- Those present at this vigil do **not** say Compline.
- **Lauds on Christmas Day** are usually said before the Dawn Mass.

---

## 18. Choice of Office and Texts (244–252)

- On a ferial day admitting an optional memoria, the Office of a saint named in the Roman Martyrology (or its approved Appendix) may be celebrated for a good reason in the normal manner of memoria (§5.4) (244).
- A **votive Office** may be celebrated, in whole or in part, for a public or devotional reason (e.g., pilgrimage, local feast, external solemnity of a saint), **except** on solemnities, Sundays of Advent / Lent / Eastertide, Ash Wednesday, Holy Week, the octave of Easter, and 2 November (245).
- On Sundays, solemnities, feasts of the Lord in the General Calendar, ferial days of Lent and Holy Week, octaves of Easter and Christmas, and ferial days of 17–24 December: **proper or appropriated texts may never be changed** (this includes antiphons, hymns, readings, responsories, concluding prayers, and often the psalms) (247).
- On other days, Sunday psalms of another week may be substituted; other psalms may be chosen (in pastoral celebrations) for the people's gradual understanding of the psalter (247).
- Continuous Office of Readings scripture must be maintained in Advent, Christmas, Lent, Eastertide; in Ordinary Time, for a good reason, readings may be selected from other days or other parts of scripture (248).
- If continuous readings are interrupted by a solemnity, feast, or special celebration, in the same week one may either join the omitted passages with those remaining, or decide which texts to prefer (249).
- Instead of the assigned 2nd reading, another reading from the same season may be chosen (250); on ordinary ferial days, a quasi-continuous reading from a single Father may be taken.
- Readings, prayers, songs, and intercessions assigned to ferial days of a particular season may be said on other ferial days of the same season (251), except where 247 applies.
- Other psalms from the same Hour of a different day may be substituted for spiritual or pastoral reason (252), subject to 247.

---

## 19. Calendar Used (241–243)

- The Office in choir or in common is celebrated according to the proper calendar (diocese, religious family, or particular Church).
- A cleric or religious obliged to the Office who participates in a celebration in common according to a different calendar or rite satisfies his obligation thereby for that part of the Office.
- In individual celebration, either the calendar of the place or one's proper calendar may be used, **except on solemnities and proper feasts**, where the proper calendar prevails.

## 20. Procedure for Determining the Office on a Given Day

Given a date, identify the contents of the Office as follows:

1. **Determine the day's class** (§4): is it Easter Triduum / a Sunday / a solemnity / a feast / a memoria (obligatory or optional) / a ferial day?
   - Apply ranking. If two observances coincide, the higher rank prevails. Only solemnities are transferable.
   - If the day is in a memoria-suppressing privileged season (§5.5), apply suppression rules.
2. **Determine the psalter week**:
   - Anchor: 1st Sunday of Advent / 1st Sunday of Ordinary Time / 1st Sunday of Lent / Easter Sunday begin Week I; cycle resumes from the week indicated in the Proper of Season after Pentecost.
3. **Determine the year of the biblical reading cycle** (Year I or II) if using the two-year supplement.
4. **Determine the Sunday gospel lectionary cycle** (Year A, B, or C) for gospel-canticle antiphons that vary by cycle (§7).
5. **For each Hour**:
   - Apply the structural rules of §3 for that Hour.
   - Choose each variable element (hymn, antiphons, psalmody, readings, responsories, intercessions, concluding prayer, Marian antiphon, etc.) by:
